# Main + Fun Tracks — Debate & Verdict

## Prompt given (excerpt)
> You are the impartial judge of the main- and fun-track debate. Read all six advocate files in full, stage the debate on paper (steelman each, head-to-head comparisons, a FinTech reality check, which fun tracks combine), rank every track without ruling any out unexplained, and recommend one main track plus fun track(s).

## 0. Verdict in brief

- **Main track: Sustainability.** Confidence is moderate, about 60%. It has the smallest pool I could verify: the identical 2025 track drew 15 of 122 MHacks submissions, and a comparable track drew 12% at LA Hacks 2025. It suits a software-strong team and matches the event's "Digital Garden" theme. It also keeps the SpaceX, Relay, Fetch.ai and FREE-WILi stacks open.
- **Fun track: Judged by an LLM (enter it).** Add **Dumbest Idea** only if the build ends up with a genuine comic hook. Skip **Useless AI** unless the team switches to a Hardware gag build.
- **FinTech: not worth it for this team.** The crowding fear is overstated, because FinTech is probably *not* the most crowded track (AI is). But FinTech gives up SpaceX, the team's top sponsor. Sustainability offers a pool at least as small and keeps SpaceX.
- **What would flip the main track:** a teammate who will own the electronics from 12:00 PM today makes **Hardware** the pick (§8).
- ⏰ Hacking runs 12 PM Oct 3 to 12 PM Oct 4. MLH hardware is "distributed on a first-come, first-served basis" ([Handbook](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/2026-Hacker-Handbook-3ca24ca0c81b80fb8adee2e26c8508af)). The hardware decision has to be made before noon.

## 2. Steelmen (each case at its strongest)

**Sustainability.** Prize money is the same everywhere, so the pool size decides the main track. Sustainability has the best-documented small pool. The 2025 track used the same text and drew 12% of MHacks submissions. Its winner was a playful FREE-WiLi device that also won a sponsor prize. No 2026 sponsor pulls teams *into* this track. The event's own branding ("Digital Garden," "nature and technology") is a direct theme match. A software team can stand out here with a small physical element (Wattson's recipe) instead of competing against robotics teams. The SpaceX brief ("Real space data goes in") can be met with satellite Earth-observation data.

**Actually Intelligent (AI).** This is the honest home for what the team will build anyway. SpaceX (Grok API) and Relay, the team's two favorite sponsors, both *require* AI. So do Fetch.ai and Photon. AI is the most feasible track for a web/AI-API team. The crowd is large but shallow: the track text itself ("not just AI for AI's sake") tells judges to filter out wrappers. A team that shows an action-taking agent with a measured baseline-vs-ours eval is in the top tier by construction. The AI advocate is also the only one honest enough to say FinTech is *less* crowded than AI.

**Beyond the Code (Hardware).** This track has the highest ceiling. Physical builds took the 2025 Grand Award, Greenprint and Portal at MHacks. They also took top overall prizes at peer events: TreeHacks 2026 Shepherd ([Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2026/02/15/12th-annual-treehacks/)) and HackMIT 2026 Peel ([The Tech](https://thetech.com/2026/10/01/hackmit-2026)). A 3-minute pitch rewards a "10-second it-moved moment." The pool self-limits because parts are scarce. "Skyward," a servo pointer tracking real satellites with a Grok Voice agent, is the most *space-native* SpaceX entry any advocate proposed.

**Useless AI.** The prize is a joke, but the project shape is the most demo-friendly way to win personality-driven sponsors (SpaceX "legendary," Relay, ElevenLabs, Photon). MHacks has rewarded an absurd premise on real engineering, including its 2025 Grand Award. Entry is free. It is the natural fit for a Hardware "useless machine."

**Dumbest Idea.** Same logic, broader door. It wants the "dumbest, funniest *hack*," which doesn't have to be pointless *AI*. That makes it compatible with an absurd premise sitting on a real engine. The "in the room" wording rewards a live laugh, which helps presentation scores with every judge who walks by. The advocate is honest that the prize is worth 2/10.

**Judged by an LLM.** This is the only fun track that rewards the same thing the main tracks do: technical depth, written down clearly. It needs no change to the project. The work it asks for (a rubric-shaped Devpost, a clear README, a measured result) is work that human judges and Fetch.ai's README and video requirements need anyway ([Fetch hackpack](https://www.fetch.ai/events/hackathons/mhacks-2026/hackpack)).

## 3. Main tracks head-to-head

### Sustainability vs Actually Intelligent (AI)
- **Where Sustainability holds:**
  - The pool-size argument is the best-sourced claim in either file. The 15-of-122 figure is my own recount. LA Hacks 2025, a pick-one-of-four event, shows the same 12% for its sustainability track (§4 table).
  - The AI advocate's own data says AI is the crowd. 47–51% of 2025 projects listed an LLM, and "agent" mentions rose from 6% to 35%. That second figure is the advocate's and I did not re-check it.
- **Where Sustainability overreaches:**
  1. **"SpaceX: Strong."** FIRMS really is satellite data ([Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms)). But the SpaceX brief emphasizes "decades of missions, and a literature no human can read all of" ([Tracks & Prizes](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/3ed24ca0c81b80579aeff03edfa88af5)). Whether its judges count Earth observation as "space data" is unverified. I rate the fit *plausible*, not strong.
  2. **"A pool a third the size of AI's."** This is inference. The Digital Garden theme could pull more teams in, and the advocate concedes this.
  3. **2025 opt-ins were not exclusive.** Ventura sits in the Greenprint list but won Overdrive. So 15 overstates real sustainability intent, which helps the case slightly.
- **Where AI holds:** its feasibility (9) is real, and both favorite sponsors are AI-native.
- **Where AI overreaches:**
  1. **"Stacking 10."** Sponsor tracks are open to every main track ("On top of your main track, submit to as many sponsor tracks as apply"). An AI-powered sustainability project can still enter Fetch.ai, Relay and SpaceX. AI's stacking edge over Sustainability is therefore small. The AI advocate effectively concedes this ("AI is a technique, not a domain… if your idea genuinely has a climate or money angle, they're right").
  2. **"Relay is awkward for Sustainability."** Wrong on the text: Relay's own idea list includes "Food: dining halls, free food," which is the obvious food-waste angle.
- **Edge: Sustainability.** It gives up little of AI's stacking and avoids AI's pool.

### Actually Intelligent (AI) vs Beyond the Code (Hardware)
- **Where AI holds:**
  - Hardware's feasibility risk is real. Parts are first-come-first-served ([Handbook](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/2026-Hacker-Handbook-3ca24ca0c81b80fb8adee2e26c8508af)), and the team's hardware experience is unknown.
  - Every recent hardware grand-prize winner the advocates cite had AI inside.
- **Where AI overreaches:** "hardware stacks with only about 1 sponsor (FREE-WILi)." Hardware's Skyward stacks SpaceX + Relay + ElevenLabs + Notability. A device can be called through Relay just like a chatbot (the Relay/LiveKit path is per the Hardware advocate and unverified end to end).
- **Where Hardware holds:**
  - The *top-tier* precedent is real and verified: Grand Award, Greenprint and Portal in 2025.
  - Demo impact is genuinely higher in a 3-minute format.
- **Where Hardware overreaches:**
  1. **The 54% win rate.** As shown in §1, it comes mostly from hardware-only sponsor prizes (MemryX ×3, FREE-WiLi). MemryX does not return in 2026.
  2. **The context changed.** In 2025, hardware projects beat *software* in themed tracks. In 2026 they will compete against each other in a dedicated track. The track is likely smaller (inference), but the advantage of standing out against software disappears inside it.
- **Edge: Hardware if someone owns the electronics; AI otherwise.** AI's odds are poor, but its floor is high. Hardware's ceiling is higher and its floor is a broken demo.

### Sustainability vs Beyond the Code (Hardware)
- **Where Hardware holds:**
  - Skyward is a more convincing SpaceX entry than Earth-observation data.
  - A dedicated hardware track probably also has a small pool. Both advocates estimate 10–20% of submissions, which is inference in both cases.
- **Where Sustainability holds:** the decisive argument is the **Wattson asymmetry**.
  - A FREE-WILi plus an IR blaster is a *highlight* in the Sustainability pool, which was full of dashboards and calculators in 2025 ([Greenprint opt-ins](https://mhacks-2025.devpost.com/submissions/search?prize_filter%5Bprizes%5D%5B%5D=90481)).
  - The same device is the *floor* in the Hardware pool.
  - Sustainability therefore captures most of hardware's demo advantage without staking the main-track result on unknown electronics skills.
- **Where Sustainability overreaches:** "Feasibility 8" assumes the optional hardware piece works. Without it, demo impact drops to roughly 4–6, as the advocate admits ("drops to 4 if it becomes a dashboard").
- **Edge: Sustainability for this team as described. Hardware for a team with a confirmed electronics owner.**

The advocate says as much: "Budgeting apps are probably the most common FinTech hackathon idea (inference)."

### Practical check on Nessie (my own test, 2026-10-03)
- `https://api.nessieisreal.com/atms?key=…` returned data.
- Plain `http://api.nessieisreal.com` **refused the connection**, so old tutorials that use http:// will fail.
- `/merchants` returned an empty list for a placeholder key.
- *Inference:* plan to seed your own customers, accounts and merchants. Every Nessie team draws on the same thin mock data, which makes differentiating harder.

### What FinTech buys and costs for *this* team
- **Gains:**
  - Capital One becomes native: a $300 Giftogram per member, about $1,200 for a team of four ([Tracks & Prizes](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/3ed24ca0c81b80579aeff03edfa88af5)).
  - It is a natural fit for the SpacetimeDB cash prize ($1,000/$500/$200; "prediction market," "portfolio sim"), Relay's "Money" idea, Photon, and Fetch.ai ("complete transactions").
  - Sandbox numbers suit Judged by an LLM.
- **Costs:**
  - **It breaks the SpaceX stack.** No advocate found a coherent finance + real-space-data project. The Dumbest Idea advocate's "Retrograde Bank" is a joke, not a FinTech main-track pitch.
  - It enters a pool roughly 1.5–2× Sustainability's (inference) with the idea category most prone to saturation.

**Verdict on FinTech: not genuinely worth it for this team, as stated.** The crowding fear is exaggerated, but Sustainability beats FinTech on pool size and keeps SpaceX. FinTech becomes the right call only if both of these hold:
1. The team drops SpaceX and makes **Capital One + SpacetimeDB + Relay** its priority stack.
2. It has a non-budgeting idea, such as fraud/security, insurance/climate risk (the Embers pattern), or a live prediction market.

## 5. Fun tracks head-to-head

### Judged by an LLM vs Dumbest Idea
- **Judged by an LLM holds:**
  - It needs no change to the project.
  - It rewards the same depth the main tracks score.
  - It strictly helps a serious entry, because the write-up it demands is also read by humans and sponsors.
- **Judged by an LLM overreaches:**
  1. "5–15 real contenders" and "the LLM probably reads the Devpost" are both inference. The model, inputs and rubric are unpublished ([Tracks & Prizes](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/3ed24ca0c81b80579aeff03edfa88af5); [Handbook](https://safe-banon-80d.notion.site/2026-Hacker-Handbook-3ca24ca0c81b80fb8adee2e26c8508af)).
  2. Its demo impact is low (4), as it admits.
- **Dumbest Idea holds:**
  - The MHacks precedent for "absurd premise, real engine" is verified: [ASI](https://devpost.com/software/artificial-sandwich-intelligence) and [Judy AI](https://devpost.com/software/judy-ai-4vc9ah).
  - A live laugh helps every judge's presentation score.
- **Dumbest Idea overreaches:**
  1. "Probably the best odds per entry at the event" is unsupported.
  2. Its flagship (T-Minus Dinner) files a joke under the AI track, against the track's "not just AI for AI's sake" text.
- **Edge: Judged by an LLM for a serious main-track entry. Dumbest Idea for a deliberately comic build.**

### Judged by an LLM vs Useless AI
- Useless AI's own advocate concedes the decisive point: it "clashes directly with the 'Actually Intelligent' main track." It also mostly hurts FinTech and works with Sustainability only as a "playful-but-useful hybrid," at which point "the Useless AI entry gets weak."
- Judged by an LLM conflicts with nothing. Its only weak pairing is Hardware, because the LLM probably can't see the device (inference).
- **Edge: Judged by an LLM, clearly.**

### Dumbest Idea vs Useless AI
- Both draw on the same small comic pool. Both advocates estimate 10–30 committed comedic projects, which is inference.
- Dumbest Idea's door is wider: it asks for the dumbest *hack*, so the AI need not be pointless. A useful project with a deliberately silly feature (the Sustainability advocate's "houseplant that roasts you") can credibly enter Dumbest Idea but not Useless AI.
- Useless AI is the better fit only for a pure gag in which the model's behavior is the punchline, such as the "Useless Box, With Tenure" on a Hardware main track.
- **Edge: Dumbest Idea.**

### Enter more than one?
Fun-track entry is free and unlimited ("optionally submit to as many bonus tracks if applicable"). The question is coherence, not eligibility.
- **Serious main-track project** (Sustainability, AI or FinTech): enter **Judged by an LLM**. Add **Dumbest Idea** only if a real comic element ships. Don't tick **Useless AI**: it won't win against built-for-purpose gags, and ticking a box that says "pointless" next to a "real problem" pitch is a small, avoidable signal risk. Whether judges see fun-track selections is unverified.
- **Comic Hardware project:** enter all three. Useless AI and Dumbest Idea fit natively, and Judged by an LLM costs only a strong write-up.

### Main tracks
1. **Sustainability.** It has the smallest *verified* pool: 12% at MHacks 2025 under identical text, and 12% at LA Hacks 2025. It is high-feasibility for a software team and is the direct match for the event theme. It keeps SpaceX (plausibly), Relay, Fetch.ai, FREE-WILi and Judged by an LLM in one story, and it lets a small physical element act as a differentiator rather than the minimum. Main risks: whether SpaceX judges accept Earth-observation data as "space data," and the nature theme pulling more teams.
2. **Beyond the Code (Hardware).** It has the highest ceiling and the best top-tier precedent at MHacks: 3 of the 5 serious MHacks-run 2025 awards. It also has the most space-native SpaceX idea (Skyward). It ranks second only because feasibility depends on skills the team hasn't confirmed and on parts handed out first come, first served from noon today. Its 54% win-rate headline is inflated by hardware-only sponsor prizes.
3. **Actually Intelligent (AI).** It is the most feasible track and natively fits SpaceX and Relay. But it is very likely the largest pool (about half of past projects were LLM-based), with one $2,500 prize and a "not just AI for AI's sake" bar. Its stacking edge is smaller than claimed because sponsor tracks are open to every main track. Choose it only if the team's best idea has no climate, hardware or money angle.
4. **FinTech.** It is not the most crowded track (that's AI), but it is likely mid-crowded (10–26% at peer pick-one events, plus Nessie, SpacetimeDB and Relay pull). It is the one main track that breaks the SpaceX stack, and its idea space saturates fastest. It ranks below AI *for this team* only because of the SpaceX preference. On pure main-track odds, FinTech beats AI. Promote it if Capital One becomes the priority sponsor (§4).

### Fun tracks
1. **Judged by an LLM.** It is the only fun track that strengthens a serious entry, it conflicts with no main track, and it costs about 1–2 person-hours of write-up that the main and Fetch.ai judging need anyway. Weaknesses: an opaque method, a mystery prize, and no stage moment.
2. **Dumbest Idea.** It has the widest comic door (any hack, not just pointless AI) and verified MHacks precedent for absurd premises on real engineering. It fits Hardware best and fits a serious entry only through a genuine comic feature. The prize is a Bop It.
3. **Useless AI.** It is the narrowest track: the AI itself must be pointless. It conflicts outright with the AI and FinTech main tracks and weakens with Sustainability. It is ranked last for this team, not dismissed: it is a strong pick only for a Hardware gag build, where it stacks with Dumbest Idea at no cost.
