# Fun Track Advocate — Dumbest Idea

## Prompt given (excerpt)
> You are the advocate for the fun track "Dumbest Idea" at MHacks 2026. Research how it is judged, how crowded it will be, what has won analogous categories, how it stacks with the fun and sponsor tracks, three project directions, honest weaknesses, and a 1–10 scorecard. Make the strongest honest case — an impartial judge will weigh your file against five other track advocates.

---

## TL;DR — the case

- **Entering Dumbest Idea is free, and you should enter it either way.** The handbook lists it as a "Side quest". It sits apart from the four $2,500 main tracks and the $5,000 grand prize, and **the prize is a Bop It** ([Tracks & Prizes][hb-prizes]). Nobody should pick this track for the prize. Its value is that it costs nothing to add, and that the same build tends to win other things.
- **Building on purpose for it is a strong, low-competition play if the joke rides on real tech.** At MHacks 2025 the fun prize ("Brainrot") went to Judy AI, a polished, working VS Code companion with Gemini chat and ElevenLabs voice ([Devpost][judy]). The **$4,000 Grand Award** went to *Artificial Sandwich Intelligence*: a deliberately absurd premise ("it's not AGI, but ASI will fully autonomously make you a sandwich") running on a real transformer robot policy ([Devpost][asi], [MHacks 2025][m25]). MHacks judges have rewarded an absurd premise on top of a real engine.
- **It fits the team's preferences and sponsor shortlist unusually well.** It pairs naturally with SpaceX "Make it Legendary", ElevenLabs, Photon, Relay and SpacetimeDB, and with the Useless AI and Judged-by-an-LLM fun tracks. It doesn't push you into FinTech.
- **The main risk is real.** A pure gag drags down a main-track score: the AI track says "not just AI for AI's sake" ([MHacks 26 Tracks][hb-tracks]). The fix is to build a serious engine under an absurd premise, and to pair it with **Beyond the Code (Hardware)** or an AI project that solves a real problem.

**Scorecard:** win probability 6 · competition 7 · feasibility 9 · demo impact 9 · stacking 8 · preference fit 9. Prize value, scored honestly, is 2.

## 1. What the track rewards and how it is likely judged

- **Grand Prize:** $5,000 cash.
- **MHacks Tracks:** "Each track awards $2,500." The table lists only the four main tracks: Sustainability, Actually Intelligent (AI), FinTech and Beyond the Code (Hardware).
- **Side quests:** the fun tracks, each with a novelty prize.

| Side quest | What you win (verbatim) |

| Useless AI | "a mystery useless prize" |
| **Dumbest Idea** | **"bop it"** |
| Judged by an LLM | "LLM decides a prize from list of prizes" |

The Instagram slide and the prize table both treat fun tracks as optional extras on top of exactly one main track ([Tracks & Prizes][hb-prizes]). *Inferred:* the prize table is the operative rule, so treat Dumbest Idea as an add-on and still pick a main track. Confirm at "Opening Ceremony: Tracks Revealed!" (10:30–11:00 AM, Oct 3) ([live site][live]).

**Judging logistics (all tracks)** ([Hacker Handbook][hb]):
- Hacking runs from 12 PM Oct 3 to 12 PM Oct 4. Submit on Devpost before then: "no late submissions or exceptions."
- Judging is on-site at the Duderstadt Center, Sunday Oct 4, 12:30–2:30 PM. "Each participating team will have a three-minute window." Projects "may be judged more than once by different judges."
- Sponsors evaluate their own tracks at the same time. "To be judged for any track, your team must be present."
- General scoring is "innovation, technical complexity, usability, and presentation quality."
- The Devpost link is "TBD" in the handbook and "Coming soon" on the live site ([Hacker Handbook][hb], [live site][live]).

**Inferred: how this specific track is judged.** No rubric for Dumbest Idea is published in the handbook as of 2026-10-03. The track text itself names the criterion: *dumbest* and *funniest*, judged *"in the room"*. That phrase suggests live reaction during the 3-minute demo matters more than the write-up. Organizers will most likely pick the winner from the main judging pass or from their own walk-around; this is unverified. Last year's analogous prize went to a project that was funny and also fully working (§3), so "works" probably counts as a tiebreaker.

## 2. Competition density

**Field size** (verified past numbers, plus an inferred estimate for 2026):
- MHacks 2025 had **380** registered Devpost participants and **122** projects. One winner took the fun Brainrot prize ([MHacks 2025][m25], [2025 gallery][m25-gal]).
- MHacks 16 (Nov 2023) had **355** participants and **100** projects ([MHacks 16][m16], [MHacks 16 gallery][m16-gal]). MHacks 15 had **368** participants ([MHacks 15][m15]).
- The 2026 site says "Join 1,000+ student builders" ([mhacks.org][site]), and teams are 1–4 people ([Hacker Handbook][hb]). *Inferred:* about **120–250 submissions**. The 1,000+ figure is marketing copy, and past Devpost registration sat near 350–380.

**How many teams will really compete for Dumbest Idea (inferred):**
- **Box-tickers:** many teams. Fun tracks are free to tick on Devpost, so plenty of earnest projects will select it. They aren't real competition, because a serious fintech dashboard isn't "the dumbest hack in the room."
- **Committed comedic builds:** few. I read all 122 MHacks 2025 taglines ([2025 gallery][m25-gal]). Only about 4 used overtly comedic framing: Artificial Sandwich Intelligence; Judy AI; MBathroom ("Have better poops"); and Wordhawk ("beat the game(pigeon)"). That's roughly 3% (my own read).
- **2026 will draw more**, because two joke side quests are advertised instead of one. My estimate is **10–30 genuinely comedic projects**, split between Useless AI and Dumbest Idea.

**Why the field stays thin:**
- The prize is a toy, so strong teams optimize for the $2,500 and $5,000 prizes and for sponsor cash.
- Most students treat a hackathon as a résumé piece and avoid "dumb" on purpose.
- Comedy is hard to pull off on no sleep.

Even so, this field is likely smaller and weaker than any main-track field. *Inferred:* the AI main track will likely draw the most entries, since 46 of 122 (about 38%) of 2025's titles and taglines mention AI, agents, LLMs, ML or Gemini (my keyword count of the [2025 gallery][m25-gal]).

2. **MHacks specifically rewarded the second archetype**, twice in 2025, including the grand prize.
3. **The best "dumb" projects are satire with a target** (Stupid Hackathon), not randomness.

## 4. Win levers

1. **A 10-second premise.** You get 3 minutes, possibly with several judges in a row ([Hacker Handbook][hb]). The premise should land as a laugh before you touch the laptop.
2. **Commit to the bit and pitch it straight.** Deliver it like a serious startup pitch. *Inferred* from the satirical tone of the strongest Stupid Hackathon projects ([Vice][vice]).
3. **Real tech under the joke.** The MHacks fun winner was a working product ([Judy AI][judy]). PennApps-style trivial wins are unlikely when 10–30 committed teams show up.
4. **A demo that makes noise or moves.** "In the room" favors voice, phones buzzing, and physical motion. Voice is cheap through ElevenLabs or the Grok Voice API ([ElevenLabs docs][el], [SpaceXAI Voice][xai-voice]).
5. **Make the judges part of the joke.** Text their phone, put them in the group chat, let them press the button. People remember what happened to them.
6. **Satire with a target.** AI hype, productivity culture, finance bros and launch-day hype are all good targets. The ASI name is a direct parody of AGI hype ([Devpost][asi]).
7. **Stay kind.** The handbook threatens expulsion for "hateful or toxic messages," and MLH's Code of Conduct applies ([Hacker Handbook][hb]). Punch up at tech culture, never at people or groups.
8. **Write a Devpost that is funny and technically rigorous.** The joke helps with human judges; the rigor helps with Judged by an LLM (§9).
9. **Stay at the table.** You must be present to be judged for any track ([Hacker Handbook][hb]).

## 6. Three concrete project directions

**Direction 1: "T-Minus Dinner", mission control for your group chat** (software; main track **Actually Intelligent**). An AI agent sits in a group chat (Photon/iMessage, or Relay, or both from one agent core). It treats every mundane plan ("boba at 8?") as a crewed launch:
- It opens a formal GO/NO-GO poll, assigns each friend a flight-controller title, and narrates a countdown.
- When someone flakes, it "scrubs" the launch, citing *real* weather rules from the most recent actual SpaceX launch ([LL2][ll2]): "NO-GO: Cumulus Cloud Rule violated."
- It generates a mission patch for every successful hangout with Grok Imagine ([Imagine][xai-img]).
- A flight director phones the latecomer in an ElevenLabs voice.

The joke is dumb, but the engine solves a real problem: coordinating a group, collecting availability, polling, and reaching a decision. That gives the AI main track a straight answer to "real problem."

- **Stacks:** Dumbest Idea, Useless AI and Judged by an LLM; plus SpaceX, ElevenLabs, Photon and/or Relay, Notability, and optionally Figma.
- **Demo:** add the judges to the chat and launch "Operation Lunch" live in 90 seconds.
- **Feasibility:** high. The work is mostly API glue plus prompt and state handling. Cursor is required for SpaceX anyway.

**Direction 2: "ISS SPOTTER 9000", a desk turret that dramatically points at the space station** (hardware; main track **Beyond the Code**):
- **The device:** a two-servo pan/tilt pointer, driven by a microcontroller or a FREE-WILi ([FREE-WILi][fw]). It computes the ISS's live azimuth and elevation from CelesTrak TLEs ([CelesTrak][celestrak]) with Skyfield ([Skyfield][skyfield]).
- **The bit:** it points at the ISS at all times, including straight *through the floor* when the station is on the other side of the planet. It loudly announces each pass in a Grok Voice "stadium announcer" voice ([Voice][xai-voice]). It shows a Grok Imagine "postcard from the crew" on screen.
- **Multiplayer layer:** everyone at the expo votes in real time on which satellite to point at next, with SpacetimeDB as the shared live state ([SpacetimeDB docs][stdb]).

Uselessly pointing at something you can't see is a classic Chindōgu-style gag ([sunhacks][sun20]). The orbital mechanics, coordinate transforms and servo calibration are real technical content for the hardware judges.

- **Stacks:** Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM; SpaceX, FREE-WILi (if used), SpacetimeDB, ElevenLabs as an alternate voice, and Figma for the voting UI.
- **Risk:** hardware experience is unknown. Hardware supply is first-come, first-served ([Hacker Handbook][hb]). Allow time for servo calibration, since real servos drift.

**Direction 3: "Retrograde Bank", the account that declines purchases based on the planets** (software; main track **FinTech** or **AI**). It uses Capital One's Nessie mock accounts and purchases ([Hacker Handbook — Nessie][hb-prizes]):
- Every transaction is "underwritten" against real planetary positions from NASA JPL Horizons ([Horizons][horizons]): "Declined — Mercury is retrograde and your coffee budget is in Scorpio."
- A disappointed ancestor phones you about it in an ElevenLabs or Grok voice, and you can argue back via Relay.
- Each statement comes with a Grok Imagine tarot-card "receipt."
- The real angle is deliberate friction on impulse spending. That answers Nessie's "improve their financial lives" requirement with a straight face.

- **Stacks:** Dumbest Idea and Useless AI; Capital One, SpaceX (planetary data plus Grok), ElevenLabs, Relay, Notability.
- **Honest caveat:** this pulls toward FinTech, the crowded track the team wants to avoid. Pick it only if Capital One is a real priority. Under the AI main track it is a weaker "real problem" story than Direction 1.

**My pick:** Direction 1. It is pure software, which suits the team. It hits the most sponsor tracks the team already favors (SpaceX, Relay, Photon, ElevenLabs), and it has a credible main-track story.

## 7. Honest weaknesses, best counterarguments, rebuttals

1. **"The prize is a Bop It. Expected value is near zero."**
   - *Concede:* true. Prize value is about 2/10.
   - *Rebuttal:* nobody should pick Dumbest Idea *instead of* a main track; that isn't allowed anyway. The question is whether building to it helps or hurts the rest of the portfolio. Entering costs nothing. A memorable absurd premise lifts presentation scores across every judge who sees the demo, including sponsors evaluating in parallel ([Hacker Handbook][hb]). The sponsor tracks it pairs with carry real value: Photon's $400 cash and interview fast-track, Relay's SF trip, and SpaceX keyboards.
2. **"A dumb project tanks the main-track score."**
   - *Concede:* a pure gag will lose the AI track, which explicitly penalizes "AI for AI's sake" ([MHacks 26 Tracks][hb-tracks]), and will score low on "usability."
   - *Rebuttal:* the 2025 MHacks Grand Award went to an openly absurd premise with real ML underneath ([Devpost][asi]). The remedy is design, not avoidance: a serious engine under an absurd premise, paired with Hardware or a real-problem AI core.
3. **"Judging is opaque and subjective."**
   - *Concede:* no Dumbest Idea rubric is published ([Hacker Handbook][hb]).
   - *Rebuttal:* that's true of all three side quests. Spread the risk by entering all three fun tracks, and make the demo objectively funny by testing it on strangers at 2 AM.
4. **"Useless AI cannibalizes it, because it's the same pool of joke projects."**
   - *Rebuttal:* they reward different things. Useless AI wants a pointless *AI*; Dumbest Idea wants the funniest *hack*, AI or not. Enter both. Whether one project can win two side quests is unverified.
5. **"Humor is high-variance, and judges are exhausted after 24 hours."**
   - *Rebuttal:* that's exactly why a 10-second laugh beats a dense technical pitch at 12:30 PM. Keep a 40-second "here's the real engineering" segment as a fallback.
6. **"Dumb projects are weak recruiting signals (for example, Photon's interview fast-track)."**
   - *Rebuttal:* the agent still ships working Spectrum and LLM integration. Creativity plus a working product is a good signal. MBathroom won a serious MLH AI prize with a comic voice ([Devpost][mbath]).
7. **"Comedy polish (voices, images, bits) eats engineering time."**
   - *Rebuttal:* voice and image generation are API calls ([ElevenLabs docs][el], [Imagine][xai-img]). The writing is the main cost, and one teammate can own it.

## 9. Combining with the other fun tracks, and the effect on a serious main-track entry

**Combine with the other fun tracks:**
- **Useless AI:** strong overlap. Any AI-driven gag (Directions 1 and 3) is a natural entry for both. *Unverified:* whether one project can win more than one side quest. Ask at the help desk or opening ceremony.
- **Judged by an LLM:** no conflict. The track wants something "technically sharp enough to impress an AI judge" ([MHacks 26 Tracks][hb-tracks]). *Inferred:* an LLM judge most likely reads the Devpost and repo, so write a precise technical section (architecture, data sources, what's real) under the comic intro. That same section helps human judges' "technical complexity" score.

**Recommendation:** enter all three fun tracks with one project. They're free, and they diversify a subjective outcome.

**Does it help or hurt a serious main-track entry?** It depends on how the project is built:
- **It hurts** if the project is *only* a joke. Main judges score "innovation, technical complexity, usability, and presentation quality" ([Hacker Handbook][hb]), and a gag forfeits usability and impact.
- **It helps** if the premise is absurd and the engine is real. The premise raises "innovation" and "presentation," and the engine carries "technical complexity." This is the 2025 Grand Award pattern ([Devpost][asi]).

**A 3-minute structure that serves both audiences:**
1. 15 s: the joke hook.
2. 90 s: a live demo with the judge participating.
3. 45 s: the real engineering.
4. 30 s: "and here's the real problem it actually solves."

**Best main-track pairings:** Beyond the Code (Hardware) first, then Actually Intelligent (AI) with a real-problem core.
