# Fun Track Advocate — Useless AI

## Prompt given (excerpt)
> You are the advocate for the fun track "Useless AI" at MHacks 2026. Research how it is judged, how crowded it will be, what has won analogous categories, how it stacks with the fun and sponsor tracks, three project directions, honest weaknesses, and a 1–10 scorecard. Make the strongest honest case — an impartial judge will weigh your file against five other track advocates.

---

## The case in one paragraph

Useless AI is not worth entering for its prize. The official handbook lists the prize as "a mystery useless prize" [H2]. It is worth entering because of what it does to the project around it. Entering costs nothing: fun tracks are optional bonus entries, and each one is a checkbox [IG, H2]. It matches the team's existing lean almost exactly. SpaceX "Make it Legendary" asks for real space data turned into something "legendary", made with Grok Imagine or Voice [H2]. Relay asks for an agent you can call or video-chat [H2]. ElevenLabs gives the best voice [H2]. All three are easiest to show off with a funny, character-driven agent. MHacks judges have recently rewarded projects that are playful on the surface and technically serious underneath. Two 2025 examples:
- The 2025 Grand Award went to "Artificial Sandwich Intelligence", a learned robot policy pitched as an "ASI" that makes you a sandwich [P2].
- The 2025 Brainrot prize went to a Gemini + ElevenLabs pixel-art companion [P1].

The honest catch: Useless AI clashes directly with the "Actually Intelligent" main track, which rejects "AI for AI's sake" [H3]. It also pulls against FinTech and Sustainability. The coherent combination is **Hardware main track + Useless AI + Dumbest Idea + Judged by an LLM + 1–3 voice/agent/space sponsor tracks**. A software-only version works too, but the team must accept weaker odds on the main track.

## 1. What the track rewards and how it will likely be judged

**Verified facts**
- **Official track text** (handbook "MHacks 26 Tracks" page): "Because not everything needs to be optimized. Build the most gloriously pointless AI application you can dream up." [H3]
- **Status: side quest, not a main track.** The handbook's Tracks & Prizes page splits prizes into three groups [H2]:
  - "MHacks Tracks": Sustainability, Actually Intelligent, FinTech and Hardware, each worth **$2,500**.
  - A **$5,000** Grand Prize.
  - "Side quests": Useless AI, Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM.
- **Side-quest prizes** [H2]:
  - Useless AI: "a mystery useless prize".
  - Dumbest Idea: "bop it".
  - Judged by an LLM: "LLM decides a prize from list of prizes".
- **Ambiguity worth confirming on site.** The handbook's "MHacks 26 Tracks" page numbers all seven tracks (1–7) under one heading: "Hackers are required to build under one of the themes" [H3]. The Instagram slides and the Tracks & Prizes page make clear that only tracks 1–4 count as main tracks [IG, H2]. **Assume Useless AI does NOT satisfy the main-track requirement.** Ask in Discord or at the help desk.
- **Judging format** [H1]:
  - Judging is on-site at the Duderstadt Center, Sunday Oct 4, 12:30–2:30 PM.
  - Each team gets a **3-minute** window, and projects "may be judged more than once by different judges".
  - Sponsors judge their own tracks at the same time.
  - "To be judged for any track, your team must be present."
  - Judges score on "innovation, technical complexity, usability, and presentation quality."
- **Deadline.** Hacking runs 12 PM Oct 3 to 12 PM Oct 4, and the project must be submitted on Devpost before 12 PM [H1]. The Devpost link was still "TBD" in the handbook [H1] and `null` on the live site when checked [H4].
- **No public rubric.** No separate rubric for Useless AI has been published. The live-site prize panel still reads "Prize details coming soon" [H4].

**Inferences (unverified)**
- **Who judges it.** Fun tracks are probably judged by MHacks organizers, or by main-panel judges as a side vote, not by sponsors. MHacks 2025's Brainrot prize had no published criteria beyond the general rubric [P0].
- **What the wording rewards.**
  - "Gloriously" points to over-commitment: real engineering spent on a pointless goal.
  - "AI application" means the AI must be central, not decoration.
  - "Most … you can dream up" means imagination and committed absurdity count most.
  - Peer events use the same idea. CTRL+HACK+DEL's "Most Useless Hack" lists absurdity, originality, humor and commitment to uselessness as its judging lens [C3]. HackPrinceton's useless-project prize celebrates "pristine code powering gloriously useless projects" [C1].
- **The general rubric still applies to some degree.** Even if it is used loosely, technical complexity and presentation still count. A gimmick with no engineering underneath is weak.

## 2. Competition density

**Data points**
- MHacks 2025: 380 registered participants and **122 Devpost submissions** [P0, P0g].
- MHacks 2024: 434 participants [P3].
- MHacks 2026 markets "1,000+ student builders" [M1]. That is a marketing claim, not a headcount.

**Estimate (inference, clearly labeled)**
- **Submissions.** 2025 had about 3.1 participants per submission. At that rate, 2026 would see roughly **150–320 submissions**, depending on real turnout.
- **How many tick the box.** Ticking a fun track is free, so "it won't hurt" ticking will be common. I estimate **15–25% of submissions** tick Useless AI, about **25–80 entries**.
- **How many are real contenders.** Only projects that are *actually* pointless AI can win. Most serious projects can't credibly argue they are useless. I estimate **10–30 genuine contenders**.

**Why it is less crowded than it looks**
- The prize is a joke [H2]. Prize-optimizers will chase the $2,500 main tracks and cash sponsor tracks.
- Most of the field is pushed toward usefulness:
  - The "Actually Intelligent" track says "not just AI for AI's sake" [H3].
  - Fetch.ai's track gives **20%** of its score to "Real-World Impact & Usefulness" [S1].
  - Capital One wants apps that "improve their financial lives" [H2].
- Most AI teams will build "useful agent" projects. That leaves the absurd lane less crowded than the general AI lane, which will be the most crowded at the event (inference).

**Why it could be more crowded**
- AI-API projects are the cheapest thing to build in 24 hours.
- Many teams will bolt on a joke persona late and tick the box.
- Those entries are weak, but they add noise.

3. **A character or interaction the judge experiences directly**, not just watches.

## 4. Win levers

1. **AI is the punchline, not a sticker.** The track is *Useless **AI***, so the joke must rely on the model's behavior: reasoning, voice, generation or perception. A pointless app with an LLM call added at the end loses to one where the AI is the joke.
2. **Over-engineering on purpose.** "Gloriously" pointless means showing the absurd effort: real data pipelines, tool calls, voice and video generation, all aimed at nothing. Precedent: ASI [P2], ShakeUR [C2], HackPrinceton's prize framing [C1].
3. **Put the judge inside the joke.** The pitch window is 3 minutes [H1]. Have the agent call the judge's phone (Relay supports agents ringing people, and voice and video calls [S2]), or text them in iMessage (Photon [S3]). Judges remember what happened to them.
4. **A persona with a voice.** Judy AI won Brainrot with an animated, voiced companion [P1]. ElevenLabs voices plug into Relay calls through an "ElevenLabs Agent bridge" [S2].
5. **A pitch that is one sentence, then escalates.** Say the inversion in the first 10 seconds and spend the rest escalating.
6. **Demo reliability.** Keep a hard-coded fallback path. The Cal Hacks 12.0 grand-prize author calls the demo the most important part and recommends a fallback for when things break [C9]. Generated video takes time (Grok Imagine runs async and you poll for the result [S4]), so **pre-generate the hero clips** and show live generation only as a bonus.
7. **Commit fully.** Use a deadpan "enterprise" landing page, fake SLAs and a mock-serious Devpost write-up. Commitment to uselessness is an explicit criterion in peer rubrics [C3].

## 6. Three concrete project directions

### A. "Mission Control for Your Mundane Life" (SpaceX + Relay + ElevenLabs; software-first, optional hardware bridge)
You call, text or video-chat a Relay agent called **Flight Director** and report a trivial problem, such as "I'm out of oat milk". It escalates the problem into a full NASA-style anomaly:
- It runs a dramatic voice "go/no-go poll" of fake console positions (Grok Voice API or an ElevenLabs voice bridged into the Relay call [S5, S2]).
- It pulls **live space data** into the call: the ISS's position right now [D2], today's near-Earth asteroids and miss distances [D1], and the next launch window [D3]. It uses them to compute a "launch window" for your grocery run.
- It sends a 10–15 s **Grok Imagine** mission-briefing video of your oat milk being launched [S4].

**Tracks:** SpaceX (real data, Cursor, Grok Imagine/Voice) [H2]; Relay (text, call and video) [H2]; ElevenLabs [H2]; Useless AI, Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM.

**Demo:** the agent rings a judge's phone mid-pitch [S2].

**Main-track bridge (optional):** a physical big red ABORT button and a status beacon (Arduino + Grove button from the MLH lab [W2]) let the team argue for Hardware. Honestly, a button alone is thin for a Hardware judge.

**Feasibility:** high for a software team. It is mostly API glue plus persona writing.

### B. "The Useless Box, With Tenure" (Hardware main track + FREE-WILi/ElevenLabs)
A modern take on Minsky's useless machine [W1]. You flip the switch, and instead of just flipping it back, an LLM persona (an aggrieved tenured professor, in an ElevenLabs voice) **demands that you justify yourself**:
- A webcam (MLH lab [W2]) watches for your hand, and the box taunts you as it reaches.
- It negotiates, cites fake "peer review" and threatens to file a grievance.
- Then a servo flips the switch off anyway.
- Escalation ladder: it gets angrier each time you try, and it keeps a public "disrespect log" on a tiny web dashboard.

**Optional SpaceX hook:** the box only lets you leave it on while the ISS is overhead (Open Notify [D2]), using Grok Voice for the professor [S5].

**Tracks:** Hardware main track, a **coherent** pairing [H3]; FREE-WILi if it is the controller or display [H2, W3]; ElevenLabs; Useless AI, Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM; optionally SpaceX.

**Demo:** the judge flips the switch.

**Risk:** hardware skill is unknown, and servos are first come, first served (4 in the MLH lab) [W2]. Mitigation: grab hardware at 12 PM sharp and keep a pre-recorded backup video.

### C. "Cosmic Group-Chat Oracle" (SpaceX + Photon iMessage + Figma)
An iMessage agent built on Photon Spectrum [S3] that joins your friend group chat. It sends unsolicited, very confident "astrophysics horoscopes" computed from **real orbital data**. Example: an asteroid passes close today [D1] while the ISS is over your hometown [D2], so it concludes "do not text your ex". It remembers each friend's past "cosmic violations" (Photon asks for context that persists across interactions [H2]). It generates a **Grok Imagine** tarot card for each prophecy [S4] in a deliberately luxurious visual style, aimed at Figma Best Design [H2].

**Tracks:** SpaceX, Photon, Figma, Notability; Useless AI, Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM.

**Demo:** add the judges to a live group chat.

**Weakness:** the weakest main-track fit of the three. It is pure software with no useful outcome, so whichever main track it lists is basically a lottery ticket.

## 9. Combining with other fun tracks, and the effect on a serious main-track entry

- **Combine with all three fun tracks.** One Devpost submission can list Useless AI, Dumbest Idea and Judged by an LLM. Fun tracks are optional and you can enter "as many bonus tracks if applicable" [IG]. Useless AI and Dumbest Idea share an audience. Judged by an LLM rewards the technical rigor and write-up quality that a "gloriously" over-engineered project already has (inference). The LLM judge's actual method is unpublished.
- **Does it help or hurt a serious main-track entry?** It depends on the main track:
  - **Hardware: helps.** There is no conceptual conflict, and the useless-machine genre is a recognized hardware idiom [W1].
  - **Sustainability: neutral to helpful,** but only as a *playful-but-useful* hybrid like Wattson [P4]. At that point it is no longer truly "useless", and the Useless AI entry gets weak.
  - **Actually Intelligent: hurts.** The track text directly rejects AI for AI's sake [H3].
  - **FinTech: mostly hurts.** The usefulness mandate [H3]. Satire is a risky pitch.
- **If the team builds a serious, useful project anyway:** ticking Useless AI is harmless but pointless, because it won't win. A useful agent with a joke persona added late is exactly the "box-ticker" entry described in section 2. **Don't contort a serious project to fit this track.** Either commit to the bit (directions A–C) or skip it and enter Dumbest Idea / Judged by an LLM only where they fit honestly.

## Recommendation for the team

1. If the team will try hardware: **Direction B**, with Hardware main + Useless AI + Dumbest Idea + Judged by an LLM + FREE-WILi/ElevenLabs, and optionally SpaceX.
2. If the team stays software-only and its real goal is the SpaceX/Relay sponsor prizes: **Direction A**. Pick a main track knowing it is secondary. Confirm with organizers whether a "theme" listing of Useless AI counts [H3].
3. Either way, keep **Notability** in the stack because it costs nothing [H2]. Do **not** add Fetch.ai, Capital One Nessie or FinchNode to a Useless AI project.
