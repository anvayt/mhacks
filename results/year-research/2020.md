## Researcher A — Winners & projects
### Prompt given (excerpt)
> Research the MHacks event(s) held in 2020. You are Researcher A (winners & projects): list every winner you can find — overall placements, category/track winners, notable sponsor prizes — with what each built, its tech, links, and why it plausibly won; then find the patterns. Researcher B covers judging and signal in parallel.

### Which MHacks events happened in 2020
- **MHacks 13 Beta**, Aug 22–23, 2020, online, 36 hours, 230 registrants on Devpost. Themes: Social Good and Health. Teams of up to 4. ([hackathon page](https://mhacks-13-beta.devpost.com/))
- It is the **only MHacks event dated 2020** in Devpost's full list of MHacks hackathons (19 total; [API listing p1](https://devpost.com/api/hackathons?search=mhacks), [p2](https://devpost.com/api/hackathons?search=mhacks&status[]=ended&page=2), [p3](https://devpost.com/api/hackathons?search=mhacks&page=3)). The list goes MHacks 12 (Oct 2019), then MHacks 13 Beta (Aug 2020), then MHacks 14 (Oct 2021). No plain "MHacks 13" exists on Devpost.
- mhacks.org from that time backs this up. The archived site bundle from Aug 26, 2020 says *"MHacks is going virtual"* and *"will be held online"* ([archived app.js](https://web.archive.org/web/20200826114449/https://mhacks.org/js/app.js)). The Wayback capture history shows that bundle did not change again until Oct 2021 ([CDX history](https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=mhacks.org/js/app.js)). *Inference:* the site was not rebuilt for any other event between Aug 2020 and MHacks 14.
- Scale: the public gallery shows about **61 projects** across 3 pages (24 + 23 + 14; page 4 is empty) ([gallery](https://mhacks-13-beta.devpost.com/project-gallery)). **30 of them carry a "Winner" badge**, because there was a 30-winner "Wolfram Award for Top 30 Hacks". So roughly half of all submissions show as "winners" on Devpost. To see real signal, look at the placements and category prizes, not the badge.
- Prize list (from the [hackathon page](https://mhacks-13-beta.devpost.com/)): 1st $400 / 2nd $300 / 3rd $200 Amazon gift cards. Six category prizes with $100 Grubhub cards each: Social Justice, Professional, Educational, Social Connectivity, Medical, and Automation (by Invensity). Sponsor prizes: Facebook "Brings the World Closer Together" (Oculus Quest per member), Google Cloud Best Use 1st and 2nd plus "Best COVID-19 App" ×5 (up to $5k cloud credits), Domain.com, and Radar. The listed "$73,480 in prizes" is mostly cloud credits and software subscriptions. *Inference:* the cash prizes were small.

Why these plausibly won (*inference*, tied to the published criteria of technical ability, creativity/UI, impact, social good and presentation; see the [hackathon page](https://mhacks-13-beta.devpost.com/)):
- **Dystic** stacked about 10 Google products into one working flow (login, then jobs, then resume, then chatbot). It aimed at a sympathetic, specific group (disabled job-seekers hit by COVID layoffs) and added accessibility features. That scores on both "social good" and "technical ability", and it swept the sponsor's prizes too.
- **we-Learn** solved a problem every student judge had felt in summer 2020: finding a concept inside a recorded lecture. It had one clear "aha" demo (search a word, the video jumps there), and the team listed "an extremely beautiful UI" among its accomplishments ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/we-learn)).
- **Sportable** was the most ambitious ML idea in the field (video form classification plus AR). But the team says outright that the classifier's *"results were inconclusive and we didn't incorporate the model in our final demo"* ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/sportable)). It still placed. *Inference:* the ambition, the honest technical writeup, and a working AR/app demo were enough.

### Patterns: why these projects won
1. **Every top-3 and category winner was framed around a timely problem, with a statistic in the writeup.** The problems were COVID layoffs, remote lectures, closed gyms, plasma shortages and student loneliness. The writeups cite figures such as 15% unemployment, 10M cases, and 61% of students lonelier. Judges scored "Social Good" as an explicit criterion ([hackathon page](https://mhacks-13-beta.devpost.com/)).
2. **Building deep on one sponsor's platform paid off twice.** Both 1st and 2nd place also took Google Cloud's top two prizes, and both made GCP the *core* of the feature, not just the hosting. ApplyAI collected **5 prizes** without placing top 3 by entering every prize it qualified for: Educational, Automation, COVID-19, Domain.com and Wolfram.
3. **A working end-to-end flow beat raw ambition, but ambition plus honesty still placed.** Dystic and we-Learn were polished, complete web apps. Sportable's ML model didn't work, and the team left it out of the demo and explained why. It still took 3rd.
4. **Solo builders won category prizes.** Plasma Desk (Medical), Pronoun Pro (Social Justice) and 100DaysOfCode (Professional) were all one-person teams. Category prizes were reachable with a focused, finished, single-feature product.
5. **Sponsor-themed prizes went to projects that matched the prize theme word for word.** Connect.us is literally a "brings the world closer" product. Hotspot is a location-alert app built on Radar's geofencing SDK.
6. **Hardware was close to absent** in this virtual edition (Android Pi only, with just the Wolfram badge). 2020 says little about the 2026 Hardware track.
7. **Cross-submission was common in the virtual era.** Sportable, LORAX, Recipeas, Landmark and StudyNest were also entered in Steel City Hacks, NHacks 2020, PeddieHacks and Hack for the People, and won prizes at some of them (see each Devpost "Submitted to" list). *Inference:* some 2020 "MHacks winners" were reused projects. Check the 2026 rules before assuming that's allowed.

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **Anchor the pitch in a concrete, current pain**, and put one hard statistic in the Devpost writeup. Every 2020 placement did this.
- **Choose one sponsor stack and build the core feature on it.** That's how the 2020 1st and 2nd place won twice. In 2026 the equivalents might be ElevenLabs (voice), Fetch.ai/Relay (agents), SpacetimeDB (real-time) or Capital One Nessie (FinTech). Use it for the main feature, not just for decoration.
- **Enter every prize you plausibly qualify for.** ApplyAI got 5 prizes from one project. Cheap extras like a real domain have historically counted.
- **Demo only what works and be honest about the rest.** Sportable placed 3rd after cutting its broken model from the demo.
- **The demo needs one clear "aha" moment** (we-Learn: search a word, the video jumps there). Polished UI was something winners highlighted themselves.
- **Don't read the Devpost "Winner" badge as signal.** In 2020, 30 of about 61 projects had one. Study placements and category prizes instead.
- **2020 gives no useful evidence for the Hardware track.** Look at in-person years for that.
