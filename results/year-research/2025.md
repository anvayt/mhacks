## Researcher A — Winners & projects
### Prompt given (excerpt)
> Research the MHacks event(s) held in 2025. You are Researcher A (winners & projects): list every winner you can find — overall placements, category/track winners, notable sponsor prizes — with what each built, its tech, links, and why it plausibly won; then find the patterns. Researcher B covers judging and signal in parallel.

### Which events happened in 2025
- **MHacks 2025** was the only MHacks-branded event I could confirm for 2025: **Sept 27–28, 2025**, Duderstadt Center, Ann Arbor. It ran 24 hours, had 380 registered participants and $39,050+ in prizes, and allowed teams of up to 4 (solo entries OK). Devpost: https://mhacks-2025.devpost.com/ (also at devpost.mhacks.org) [S1][S3].
- Wikipedia lists only Sept 27–28 for 2025. The previous events were Apr 12–14, 2024 (the "MHacks x Google" AI hackathon) and Sept 28–29, 2024 [S5][S6]. MLH's 2025-season event list has no MHacks entry for Jan–Jul 2025 [S7]. I found no spring 2025 event.
- The Devpost gallery has **122 submissions**. **30 projects won 32 prize slots** (Wattson and MobiLens each won two), so roughly 1 in 4 submissions won something [S2].
- The 2025 MHacks-run tracks were: **Greenprint** (Sustainability), **Lifeline** (Healthcare), **Overdrive** (Optimization: "algorithms, automation, and design strategies") and **Portal** (Frontier Interfaces: "voice, gesture, brain-computer links, or immersive experiences"). There was also a fun **Brainrot** prize. Judging was "in-person science fair style" on four criteria: Innovation, Technical Complexity, Usability and Adherence to Theme [S3]. Researcher B covers judging in depth.

Team members below are listed exactly as they appear on each Devpost page, including handles.

**Why these plausibly won (inference):**
- **Grand Award.** It is the only winner that trained and deployed a learned robot policy on physical hardware, and it cites the ACT paper (arXiv 2304.13705). Judges could watch a working robot arm demo, compared against a human, at the science-fair table. It scores high on Technical Complexity and Innovation, and the "automate sandwich shops" pitch gives it a business story. The Hugging Face profile shows separate ~51.7M-parameter policies named BREAD1, TOMATO, TOAST2 and LETTUCE, each trained on 25-episode SO-101 datasets, all updated Sept 28, 2025. My inference is that the build chains one policy per ingredient [S8].
- **Track winners mostly used physical devices.** Wattson used FREE-WiLi plus a camera, Dementia Assistant used Spectacles and ScreenWave was a sensor glove. The exception is Ventura, an unfinished solo web app that admits its auth and LLM integration failed. That suggests a clear problem framing that fits the theme ("stop re-asking the same AI questions → less compute") can beat completeness in a thinner category (inference).
- **Brainrot** went to a playful, character-driven product that still worked (Gemini chat and ElevenLabs voice).

### 3. Patterns (counts from the 30 winners above; interpretation is inference)
1. **Physical hardware dominates the MHacks-run awards.** Of the 6 MHacks-run prizes, 4 went to hardware or wearable projects: a robot arm, a FREE-WiLi device, Spectacles and a sensor glove. Across all 30 winners, about 14 use physical hardware or wearables. The Grand Award is the most technically ambitious hardware build in the field.
2. **Sponsor-tech prizes are most of the winnable surface.** 26 of the 32 prize slots were sponsor or MLH prizes. Nine winning projects were built on hardware sponsors brought to the event (FREE-WiLi ×2, MemryX ×3, Spectacles ×4), and two of them won a second prize as well: Wattson took Greenprint and FREE-WiLi, MobiLens took Fetch.ai and Snap.
3. **Stacking several sponsor tools in one project works.** MobiLens combined Snap, Fetch.ai and Base44. Ticketry combined AgentMail, Auth0 and Gemini. Dill.study combined Gemini, AgentMail and a GoDaddy domain. Splitr lists "integrated multiple sponsor tracks" as an accomplishment. Gemini appears in 11 of 30 winners and AgentMail in 5.
4. **Agents and MCP were the main software theme in 2025.** Examples: the three Solana MCP winners, the three AgentMail winners, the three Fetch.ai winners, Conductor (Claude tool use) and CLAI.
5. **Accessibility and health framing recurs.** Dementia Assistant, SoundSense, MobiLens, Gestura and Conversenses each give a concrete person and a concrete problem, often a family member or the Deaf community.
6. **Solo teams and unfinished builds can win.** Six winners list a single member (the Grand Award, Ventura, SoundSage, SoundSense, GreenPrint and Dune MCP). Ventura (missing auth and LLM) and GreenPrint (not deployed) won while admitting gaps, which fits science-fair judging that rewards a demo you can explain over completeness.
7. **Fun and joke prizes reward polish plus personality.** Judy AI had a character-driven experience that actually worked. MBathroom won an MLH AI prize with a funny but useful campus app.

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **To compete for the Grand Award, build something physical that visibly works.** The 2025 winner was one person, a $200 robot arm and a policy trained with real ML at the event. Bring a short side-by-side or benchmark video, as that winner did.
- **Pick one main track and two or three sponsor prizes that the same build can satisfy.** Two 2025 teams won two prizes each, and many winners stacked sponsor tools. Repeat sponsors in 2026 (Fetch.ai, FREE-WILi) have public 2025 winners you can study.
- **Use loaner hardware from sponsors' tables and workshops.** Gestura came directly out of the at-event FREE-WiLi workshop, and every FREE-WiLi and MemryX prize went to a hardware hack.
- **Agents are expected, so make them do something real.** That means sending email, moving money, controlling devices or filing issues. The 2025 agent winners all closed a loop in the real world. Relay, Photon iMessage and ASI:One reward the same thing in 2026.
- **Frame the project around a specific person and problem.** Five winners led with accessibility or health. In 2026 that framing points to FinchNode HealthTech rather than a main track.
- **Scope ruthlessly, and say what didn't work.** Ventura and GreenPrint won despite missing pieces, because the core demo and the story held up.
- **For a fun track, make it both funny and finished.** Judy AI and MBathroom both had a polished, working experience.

### 8. Analysis: which judging signal is most actionable for 2026
1. **Sponsor rubrics are published, weighted, and mostly decided by the sponsor-specific line** (MemryX Differentiation 30%, Fetch.ai Use of tech 20% plus hard eligibility gates). Of the ~32 prize slots in 2025, 26 were sponsor or MLH prizes (Researcher A's count). Two 2025 sponsors are back in 2026 (**Fetch.ai, FREE-WILi**), and their 2025 briefs and winners are public. **This is the most actionable signal:** read each target sponsor's rubric before writing code, and meet every hard requirement (Agentverse registration, Chat Protocol, README badges, 3–5 min video).
2. **Workshops are a hidden gate and an idea source.** Base44's challenge was given only to workshop attendees, and Gestura's winning idea came from the FREE-WILi workshop [R8][R12].
3. **Being present at your table is part of the score.** In 2025 that meant a 2-hour science fair across 122 tables. If MDredd runs in 2026, absences become strikes and 3 in a row removes you [R14].
4. **The MHacks rubric rewards real-world need, accessibility, and robustness.** Presentation is weighted lightly on paper (5–15% in sponsor rubrics), but at a science fair the demo is the only evidence judges see (*inference*).

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **Pick the sponsor prizes first and build to their rubric.** For Fetch.ai, assume 2025's weights carry over (*inference*): functionality 25 / Fetch tech 20 / innovation 20 / impact 20 / UX & presentation 15. Register agents on Agentverse with the Chat Protocol, use ASI:One as the reasoner, put the agent addresses and badges in the README, and record a 3–5 min video.
- **Enter one main track plus every sponsor prize the same build honestly satisfies.** 2025 allowed unlimited sponsor entries, and stacked projects won two prizes.
- **Go to the sponsor workshops (FREE-WILi especially).** They are where gated challenges and winning ideas came from in 2025.
- **Stay at your table for the whole judging window**, with the demo running and a 60–90 s pitch ready. Pairwise judging may count absences as strikes.
- **Show numbers and a closed loop:** a benchmark against a human, latency or accuracy figures, real actions taken (issues filed, money moved, device controlled). The 2025 winners' write-ups lead with these.
- **Name the user and the accessibility or impact angle.** It maps directly to MHacks' Innovation and Usability sub-criteria.
- **For Useless AI / Dumbest Idea, make the joke fully work and give it personality.** The 2025 Brainrot winner was a polished, voiced companion.
- **Expect sponsor reps (often founders and engineers) to judge their own prizes** (*inference*). Speak to the specific tech, not just the pitch.
