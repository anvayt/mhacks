# MHacks 2023 — Past Winners Research

Two researchers covered this year in parallel, each with a different focus: **Researcher A** (winners & projects) and **Researcher B** (judging & signal). Part of the MHacks 2026 strategy research — see `../SUMMARY.md`.

### Patterns: why these won
1. **Technical depth on a relatable problem takes 1st.** DECO.ai put NeRF research into "rearrange my room". LumiGUI paired real hardware with a hospital hygiene problem. Both top winners went past an API wrapper into something hard (NeRF synthesis, sensor-to-database integration). *(Inference.)*
2. **Developer tools win with developer judges.** Terminal.AI (2nd plus Warp), docuMint (ML/AI) and App² (GitHub) are all tools judges could picture using the next day. Terminal.AI was installable from PyPI.
3. **A demo you can watch beats a demo you have to explain.** VSAT (VR), MotionSurfer (body-controlled game), DECO.ai (3D room) and Quick Action (AR) all show well on camera or at a table. MotionSurfer's team explicitly credits judge and mentor attention.
4. **GenAI was everywhere, so it didn't set teams apart. Grounding did.** 14 of the 24 MHacks 16 winners call an LLM (GPT, UM-GPT or OpenAI). Counting WolvWealth's DALL-E and ZenStock's HuggingFace NLP brings it to 16. Several winners stand out by grounding the LLM in real data instead of raw chat: Pinpoint Ai (vetted medical PDFs), OneVote (live search, explicitly to avoid stale LLM answers), Canvassist and Smart Office Hub (course data), Sapphiric (your PDFs). *(The count is my tally from each project's Built With and description fields.)*
5. **Prize stacking.** Terminal.AI (2nd + Warp), ALERT (Hardware + UM ITS), MyNewsWire (3rd + MongoDB) and Quick Action (AR/VR + Fidelity) each won two prizes with one project, because they used sponsor tech in a way that mattered to the product.
6. **Sponsor-API tracks reward direct, central use.** ZenStock used Capital One's Nessie API and won Best Financial Hack. Two other Capital One winners didn't use Nessie but had real finance logic (Plaid + GPT-4 accounting; PyPortfolioOpt optimization). Hedera, MongoDB, Streamlit and Google Cloud winners all made the sponsor tech load-bearing.
7. **Social impact with a specific user group.** CogniCare (Alzheimer's), HealthMS (MS), HopeHire (kidney patients), SafeBlurr (journalists and activists) and Pinpoint Ai (ER misdiagnosis) all name a narrow group of people the project helps, not just "society".
8. **Beginners and first-years win.** VSAT came from first-years, MyNewsWire was the team's first hackathon, and half of the Canvassist team were first-timers. Polish and scope discipline mattered more than experience. *(Inference from the teams' own write-ups.)*
9. **Deployed or live links are common:** mynewswire.tech, wolvwealth.tech, aslevo.tech, Streamlit/Cloud Run demos, PyPI. Aipeiron says it fixed latency specifically "that enabled smooth product demonstrations".

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **To win overall, pick one technically hard core (2023: NeRF; today, e.g., on-device CV or a real agent loop) and point it at a problem everyone has.** DECO.ai's formula: research-grade tech plus an everyday use case plus a visual demo.
- **"AI" alone won't stand out.** Most 2023 winners already used an LLM. Ground it in specific data (course data, vetted docs, live search, bank transactions) and say why that beats plain ChatGPT, as Pinpoint Ai and OneVote did.
- **Build for stacking.** Choose one main track plus one or two sponsor tracks whose tech is load-bearing. Examples: FinTech + Capital One Nessie (ZenStock's path), HealthTech + an AI track (CogniCare), Hardware + an AI track (ALERT).
- **Developer tools are a strong bet for the AI track.** Developer judges know the pain. Ship something installable (pip/npm/extension) the way Terminal.AI did.
- **Hardware is under-contested.** In 2023 the hardware winners were ALERT (Fitbit plus eye tracking) and LumiGUI (Arduino + 3D print, 1st overall at MHacks 15). For "Beyond the Code", a small but working physical prototype goes a long way.
- **Make the demo watchable in 30 seconds**: VR, body-controlled games, 3D scenes, live dashboards. Make it fast, since Aipeiron optimized latency specifically for the demo.
- **Name the user.** Social-impact winners targeted Alzheimer's patients, kidney patients, journalists and ER doctors. "Helps people" is weaker.
- **Have a live URL or package.** Several winners shipped .tech domains, Streamlit or Cloud Run demos, or PyPI packages.

### Which judging signal is most actionable for 2026
**The 3-minute, repeated pitch at your table, scored against a published rubric, with sponsor judges circulating at the same time.** This is the most actionable signal. Three facts point to it: (a) the rubric was public before hacking began (Innovation, Technical Complexity, Usability, Theme fit, plus "presentation quality"); (b) every team got only about 3 minutes, several times, from different judges; (c) sponsor judges scored their own tracks in parallel, and about a quarter of submissions won something. Together these mean a team controls most of its odds through three choices: one required main track, one or two sponsor tracks whose tech is load-bearing, and a pitch-and-demo rehearsed to land each rubric line in under 3 minutes. Caveat: the 2026 format may differ, so check the 2026 Devpost and Hacker Handbook when they come out.

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **Write the pitch against the rubric.** Spend about 30 seconds on each of problem/Innovation, live demo/Usability, "what's hard under the hood"/Technical Complexity, and why it fits the track. Close with the future plan, since judges were told to ask about process, challenges and future plans.
- **Expect to pitch 3+ times to different judges, and keep it consistent.** Every pitch should be equally sharp, so have a scripted 3-minute version and a 60-second fallback.
- **Make the demo instant.** Aipeiron did performance work just to fit the demo window. Pre-load data, cache API calls, and have a recorded backup.
- **A demo that draws a crowd helps at table judging.** MotionSurfer (body-controlled game), VSAT (VR) and DECO.ai (3D room) were all watchable at a glance.
- **Stack tracks deliberately.** 2023 allowed one theme plus company tracks, and 2026 has the same structure. Pair a main track with sponsors whose tech you actually use: FinTech + **Capital One Nessie** (ZenStock was the 2023 precedent), or AI + a sponsor's agent or voice platform.
- **Match the sponsor's own product.** Terminal.AI, a terminal tool, won Warp's prize. The UM ITS winners used ITS's Canvas data and UM-GPT. App² made GitHub the product for the GitHub prize. Read each 2026 sponsor's prize text and build toward the behavior it names.
- **Some sponsor prizes are recruiting channels.** Caterpillar offered a guaranteed interview, and the organizers said sponsors were there to meet internship and full-time candidates. Put your names and roles on the Devpost and in the pitch.
- **Write a full Devpost:** problem, how it was built, challenges, what's next, and a video. Winners' write-ups ran longer (inference), and the rubric rewards explaining technical depth that a judge can't see in 3 minutes.
- **First-timers do win.** MotionSurfer, MyNewsWire and Pedagora all came from first-hackathon teams. Scope tightly and polish one flow.
