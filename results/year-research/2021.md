### Patterns (why these won)
Counts are from the 12 winning Devpost pages above. Interpretations are marked as inferences.

1. **Use the sponsor's API to stack prizes.** 4 of 12 winners won **two** prizes: SunLite (1st + Auth0), MCall (3rd + Twilio), Fastr Food (Beginner + GCP 2nd) and Study (ML + Education). Half of the 16 prize slots were sponsor/MLH API prizes. Twilio appears in 4 winners (SunLite, MCall, Smart Lock, F.L.U.D.D) and AssemblyAI in 3 (MCall, ASL, Smart Lock). 8 of 12 used some Google product (GCP, Firebase, Maps or Vision).
2. **Hardware stood out even online.** 4 of 12 winners had physical builds (SunLite bulb, Smart Lock, Cosmic Cleaner servos, F.L.U.D.D sensors), including **1st place**. The SunLite team called hardware "an extra layer of complexity". *Inference:* a visible physical effect reads well on a demo video.
3. **Social-good framing tied to a real, often local, problem.** These include sleep health, student mental health (with a Michigan Daily statistic), ML bias, SE Michigan basement floods, Great Lakes water, ASL accessibility and radiology workload. That matches the "Social Good" criterion the Core Team judged on ([Devpost](https://mhacks-14.devpost.com/)).
4. **Every winner had a demo video.** All 12 winning pages embed a YouTube demo. The 1st-place team says one member "narrated and edited the entire pitch video". Study won two prizes with an essentially empty write-up, so *inference:* the video and demo mattered far more than the Devpost text.
5. **Solo builders could win.** By first-person write-ups or single listed members, about 5 of 12 winners appear to be solo: Shadow Clone (2nd), Brain Tumor, Resume Architect, F.L.U.D.D and Smart Lock. *Inference:* the small field (59 projects) made category prizes reachable.
6. **The bar was finished-and-clear, not perfect.** Resume Architect's hosting didn't work and Shadow Clone supported only one algorithm, yet both won. Polish went into the UI, the story and the video.
7. **Custom `.tech` domains everywhere** (SunLite, Water Monitor, Smart Lock, Resume Architect). That was MLH's Domain.com push, and it's a cheap way to look polished.

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- **Weight 2021 lightly.** It was online, had about 164 Devpost participants and 59 projects, offered $2,400 in prizes and was judged by the Core Team. MHacks 2026 is a bigger, sponsor-judged event. The patterns still match later years, though.
- **Plan prize stacking on purpose.** Pick one main track, then wire 1–2 sponsor tools (Photon, ElevenLabs, Neon, SpacetimeDB, etc.) into the core flow, not bolted on. In 2021, a third of winners double-dipped this way.
- **A small physical effect plus a clean web/SMS interface** won 1st. For **Beyond the Code (Hardware)**, think SunLite or F.L.U.D.D scale: one actuator or a few sensors, plus a messaging alert (now a natural **Photon iMessage** fit).
- **Anchor on a concrete, local, human problem** (campus lines, Michigan floods, Great Lakes, student mental health), and quote a real statistic like MCall did.
- **Treat the demo video and live demo as the product.** Every winner had one, and a near-empty write-up still won twice.
- **Sustainability and HealthTech have precedent.** Water Monitor and F.L.U.D.D map to Sustainability. The Brain Tumor tool and MCall map to FinchNode HealthTech.
- No 2021 winner maps to **FinTech**, the fun tracks, or the LLM-judged track. These categories didn't exist then.

### Judging criteria (Devpost, verbatim names, no weights given)
| Criterion | Devpost's definition (condensed) |
|---|---|
| Technical Ability | Did the team challenge itself, use its tech effectively, and build something extensible? |
| Creativity | *Defined entirely as design:* is it aesthetically pleasing (UI) and intuitive to use (UX)? |
| Impact | Does the project accomplish its goal well? |
| Social Good | Does it tackle a real-world problem with a valid, scalable, sustainable solution? |
| Presentation | Did the presenters communicate the idea clearly? |

Source: [Devpost overview](https://mhacks-14.devpost.com/). Note that MHacks' "Creativity" criterion was really a **UI/UX polish** criterion, and **Social Good** was a criterion of its own. The Leading Learners education prize had its own checklist: the problem must reach over 1M people aged 15–30+, the entry must be a mobile or desktop app, it must include a Figma/InVision/Canva prototype, and the design must be "user-friendly". It was marked "Non-technical" ([Devpost](https://mhacks-14.devpost.com/)).

### Which judging signal is most actionable for 2026
1. **"Would the judge personally use this?"** The judges' most specific praise was first-person: "I would definitely use this" (1st place) and "I wish I had this myself" (MLH). An everyday pain point a student judge has felt beat abstract ambition. This carries over to any format.
2. **Be well-rounded, not spiky.** "Well-rounded" was used for both 1st and 3rd place. With five unweighted criteria (tech, UI/UX, works, social good, presentation), being solid on every one beat being brilliant on one. *(Inference.)*
3. **Count UI polish as a scored criterion.** MHacks defined "Creativity" as UI/UX, and 2nd place was praised for a "beautiful" UI.
4. **The demo artifact is judged, not just the code.** Every winner had a video, and the MLH judge picked out a video filmed in real life over a screen recording. In 2026 the demo is in person, so the same rule becomes: show a physical, visible effect.
5. **Caveat:** 2021 was online, small (164 registrants, 59 projects), and judged asynchronously by students on the core team. Use it for *what judges value*, not for *how judging runs* in 2026.

### Takeaways for MHacks 2026
- Pick a problem the judges themselves have, such as waking up, dining-hall lines, flooding or student stress, and say so in the pitch. That was the most repeated praise in 2021.
- Aim for well-rounded: working end to end, a clean UI, a clear social-good or impact line, and a tight pitch. Don't sink 36 hours into one impressive but fragile piece.
- Treat UI/UX as points, not decoration. That fits the **Figma best design** sponsor track too.
- For **Beyond the Code (Hardware)**: in 2021, 4 of 5 hardware submissions won something, including 1st. A small, visible physical effect (a bulb, servos, sensors that trigger an alert) is enough.
- Wire sponsor tools into the core flow, the way SunLite used Twilio and Auth0. Sponsor and MLH judges rewarded integration and real-world framing, and doubled-up wins came from this.
- Even with live judging, record a short demo video **filmed in real life** as a backup and for Devpost. Every 2021 winner had one.
- Make the project title and one-line pitch distinctive. *Inference:* a judge mixed up two flood/water projects on stage, and a memorable name protects you when judges go through dozens of projects.
- Novelty tracks (Useless AI, Dumbest Idea, Judged by an LLM) have no 2021 precedent, so 2021 gives no signal for them.
